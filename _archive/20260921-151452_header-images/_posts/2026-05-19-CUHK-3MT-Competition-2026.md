### About 3MT®

The Three Minute Thesis (3MT®) competition was founded at The University of Queensland in 2008. Research postgraduate students present their thesis to a general audience in **three minutes**, using **a single static slide**. At CUHK, the 2026 competition was organised by the Graduate School, the Independent Learning Centre (ILC) and the CUHK Library.

- **Date:** Tuesday, 19 May 2026, 2:15–5:30 pm
- **Venue:** VR Zone, Learning Garden, University Library, The Chinese University of Hong Kong

### My Talk

#### Where Young People Move and Why: Mapping Youth Migration across Chinese Cities with Geotagged Weibo Check-ins

How many of us have moved to a new city for a degree, a job or a fresh start? Millions of young people in China make this choice every year. Yet China's census, the main tool for studying migration, is taken only once every ten years, and surveys rely on small samples. My research offers a faster alternative: geotagged check-ins from about 70,000 young Weibo users, corrected for platform bias against census benchmarks, reveal how young people move between cities. Three messages stand out:

- **Concentration:** after the pandemic, youth migration became more concentrated in the three coastal megaregions (Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei, the Yangtze River Delta and the Pearl River Delta).
- **What really pulls them:** universities and knowledge-intensive jobs attract young migrants nearly three times more strongly than income alone, and poor air quality can cancel out a high paycheck.
- **Digital networks shrink distance:** young people with stronger online networks are less constrained by distance, especially young women.

Cities that want to attract young talent need more than subsidies: knowledge-based jobs, cleaner environments and inclusive communities for newcomers.
