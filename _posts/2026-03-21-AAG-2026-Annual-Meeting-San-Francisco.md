### Presentation

#### Mapping Population Mobility and Urban Hierarchy in China through Social Media Check-ins: Bias-Corrected Intercity Migration Flows across Chinese Cities

**Abstract:** Censuses and surveys, the main sources for migration research, are updated infrequently and often lack spatial detail. Geotagged check-ins on Sina Weibo provide timely location signals, but platform users are younger, more urban and more active online than the general population. This study corrects these demographic biases against census benchmarks and estimates intercity migration flows across 337 Chinese prefecture-level cities from 2021 to 2024. The corrected flows show that China's post-pandemic migration network did not simply return to its previous state but adapted in a resilient way: national centres such as Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Beijing and Hangzhou remained dominant, while secondary cities in the Yangtze River Delta gained importance and functional urban regions became more integrated across administrative boundaries.
